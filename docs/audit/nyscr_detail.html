II. Scope of Work:

A. Introduction and Background
1. Stony Brook Medicine (SBM} is advancing its sustainability and decarbonization initiatives through a combination of utility system electrification and energy conservation measures intended to reduce facility energy consumption and reliance on fossil fuels.
2. As part of these efforts, SBM has been awarded funding from the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA) for the implementation of an industrial heat pump demonstration project. The project is intended to reduce both chilled water plant demand and hot water reheat energy consumption by recovering waste heat from the hospital chilled water system and transferring it to the reheat hot water system.
3. The NYSERDA grant includes specific project schedule, reporting, documentation, monitoring, and verification requirements that must be incorporated into the project execution strategy and ultimately satisfied upon project completion.
B. Project Objectives
The primary objectives of the project are to:
1. Reduce overall hospital energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions.
2. Recover thermal energy from the chilled water return system through the application of industrial-grade heat pump technology.
3. Reduce the hospital's hot water reheat demand by utilizing recovered waste heat.
4. Demonstrate the technical and economic viability of large-scale heat recovery within an operational healthcare facility.
5. Satisfy all NYSERDA grant requirements, including project documentation, monitoring, and verification activities.
C. Scope of Professional Services
SBM seeks qualified professional engineering firms to provide all necessary design and construction phase services for the implementation of an electrically driven, industrial-grade, water-to-water heat pump system. The selected consultant shall provide professional services necessary to evaluate, design, document, and support the installation of a heat recovery system that extracts thermal energy from the hospital chilled water return piping downstream of the Network N-8 Mechanical Room and transfers that energy to the hospital reheat hot water system.
The Consultant's services shall include, but not be limited to:
1. Site investigation and verification of existing conditions.
2. Engineering analysis and system performance evaluation.
3. Development of design criteria and basis-of-design documentation.
4. Preparation of schematic, design development, and construction documents.
5. Coordination with SBUH operations staff and NYSERDA requirements.
6. Preparation of equipment specifications and procurement support documentation.

7. Construction administration and engineering support services during construction and closeout.
8. Review of contractor submittals and requests for information.
9. Commissioning support, startup verification, and system performance validation.
10. Support for monitoring, measurement, verification, and project documentation required under the NYSERDA grant.

D. Project Requirements
1. The proposed system shall provide approximately 1,100 tons of cooling capacity and be sized to maximize utilization of recovered thermal energy for the hospital reheat system.
2. The heat pump installation shall consist of multiple packaged heat pump units, each incorporating a minimum of two compressors. The system design shall provide operational flexibility, reliability, redundancy, maintainability, and resiliency appropriate for a critical healthcare facility environment.
3. The Consultant shall evaluate system integration requirements, utility impacts, controls modifications, phasing considerations, and operational constraints necessary to maintain uninterrupted hospital operations throughout project execution.
